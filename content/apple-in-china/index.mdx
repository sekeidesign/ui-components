In his investigative nonfiction, Patrick McGee tells the story of Apple's manufacturing woes and how the decades-long search for solutions led to the tech giant's global success. He recounts attempts to manufacture in the USA, followed by a split between Ireland and Singapore, an unsuccessful stint in Japan, and hard-earned success in China.

The book's core thesis is that Apple, with its thousands of engineers and millions of person-hours in the country, was a decisive though almost unintentional actor in China's electronics manufacturing success. There's no question that Foxconn and the myriad other manufacturing giants owe Apple much of their billion-dollar fortunes, but what makes this one of my favourite books is a different and perhaps subtler lesson.

McGee returns repeatedly to the relentless obsession of Jony Ive and the Product Design team to achieve perfection and innovation fueled a search for only the best manufacturing partners. At the time, this was no easy feat. China had yet to become the center of tech and electronics manufacturing that it is today, so the Operations and Manufacturing Design teams had to scour the world for suppliers who showed an unwavering devotion to a level of manufacturing excellence that could match that of Apple. 

Foxconn's skillset at the time had a long way to go, but it was immediately clear that founder Terry Gou would stop at nothing to deliver Apple's designs to spec, happily taking losses on contracts to perfect their craft. Gou knew that the what he would learn from working closely with Apple engineers would be far more valuable than the short term profits, and the bet paid off.

What I love about this story, as a product designer, is the practical proof of just how important it is to have a strong vision. To seek absolute excellence not only in your own work, but in the work of those who surround you. To experiment and innovate along an entire supply chain in the pursuit of something few will truly appreciate, but that many will, at least subconsciously, love and admire.
